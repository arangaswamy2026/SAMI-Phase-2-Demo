Universal AI Alert Triage Patterns

Common design patterns observed across all vendors — cybersecurity and non-cybersecurity

Signal Compression

Every mature AI triage product collapses raw events into grouped incidents. PagerDuty reports 87–98% noise reduction. CrowdStrike claims 98% alert reduction. The raw event list is never the user's entry point.

Business Context Injection

AI attaches impact language to every surface incident — number of affected assets, users at risk, ARR exposure, SLA breach risk. Users see consequence, not just event. This is the primary difference between SIEM and modern AI triage.

Embedded Rationale

The AI explains why something was flagged, inline. Darktrace, CrowdStrike, and Microsoft Sentinel all show AI reasoning alongside the alert card, not on a separate page. Trust is built in the triage view.

Contextual Actions

Actions are specific to the alert type. Not a generic "View" button — instead "Deploy patch", "Force MFA", "Isolate host", "Run playbook". The action is the recommendation, not just a link to more information.

Triage Has Already Happened

Users arrive to decisions, not events. The AI has pre-scored, pre-grouped, and pre-prioritized before the analyst lands. The screen communicates "here is what needs your attention" — not "here is everything that happened."

Root Cause Linkage

Related events are visually connected. Microsoft Sentinel's investigation graph, Dynatrace's root cause tree, PagerDuty's Probable Origin — all surface why an alert chain exists, not just each individual alert in isolation.

Key Findings

What the competitive landscape tells us about AI alert triage design

01

The raw event list is never the primary surface

Every mature platform — from CrowdStrike to PagerDuty to Dynatrace — has moved the raw event log to a secondary or tertiary view. The primary surface is AI-grouped incidents or problems. SonicWall's current Notifications tab is the equivalent of what these platforms removed 3–5 years ago.

02

Flapping alerts are auto-suppressed — not listed

PagerDuty's Auto-Pause detects and suppresses transient/flapping alerts using ML. Dynatrace Davis never shows repeated interface up/down events as separate problems — they're correlated into one. The NSM Notifications tab currently shows 10+ repeated "interface X3 up/down" rows as separate entries.

03

AI rationale is inline — not a separate AI tab

Every product shows the AI's reasoning inside the alert card itself. CrowdStrike embeds the Detection Triage Agent result in the same card. Darktrace shows investigation rationale inline. SAMI's current SAMI badge approach is directionally correct — but the content depth varies across cards.

04

Business context is what differentiates mature products

Microsoft Sentinel and Palo Alto still focus primarily on technical severity. CrowdStrike and Darktrace are adding business impact language. SAMI is actually ahead of most competitors here — surfacing ARR at risk, tenant counts, and device blast radius is a genuine differentiator that should be expanded, not reduced.

05

Multi-tenant context is a genuine white space

None of the reviewed products have a native multi-tenant AI triage surface. CrowdStrike, Darktrace, and Palo Alto are all single-tenant architectures. SAMI's ability to surface "4 tenants, 12 firewalls affected" in a single alert card is a capability no competitor has replicated. This is the strongest design differentiator in the current SAMI concept.

06

"Snooze" on behavioral anomalies is a design anti-pattern

No competitor puts a Snooze button at the same visual priority as an action button on high-severity behavioral alerts. CrowdStrike requires human-in-the-loop approval for impactful actions. Microsoft Sentinel requires explicit status changes. SAMI's current Snooze CTA on the credential stuffing card creates a design risk — both operationally and in terms of perceived seriousness.

SAMI Design Implications — What to Borrow, What to Avoid, What to Defend

↑

Borrow: Flapping alert suppression (PagerDuty Auto-Pause)

SAMI should auto-suppress repeated interface up/down events that historically self-resolve. PagerDuty uses ML to learn which alert patterns are transient — SAMI should apply the same logic to SonicOS interface state events. This alone would eliminate ~80% of the noise in the current NSM Notifications tab.

↑

Borrow: Probable Origin / Root Cause Linking (PagerDuty, Dynatrace, Sentinel)

SAMI Need Attention cards should show "triggered by" linkage — e.g., "3 gateways offline likely caused by BGP route change at Delta Logistics." Dynatrace Davis and PagerDuty Probable Origin both surface root cause candidates automatically. This would reduce the triage burden significantly for MSP partners.

↑

Borrow: Incident Timeline / Attack Chronology (Microsoft Sentinel)

When a SAMI alert spans multiple events over time, a timeline view inside the expanded card would significantly increase triage confidence. Microsoft Sentinel's Incident Timeline widget is the gold standard — showing alerts and bookmarks on a visual timeline within the incident page.

→

Defend: Business context with ARR, tenant counts, device blast radius

SAMI's "$287K ARR at renewal risk" and "4 Tenants · 12 Firewalls" language is more mature than most competitors. CrowdStrike and Darktrace do not surface ARR or MSP-level business context. This is a genuine differentiator — expand it, do not simplify it away in refinement.

→

Defend: Multi-tenant grouping in a single alert card

No competitor surfaces cross-tenant impact in a single alert. "Acme Corp, Beta Networks, Westfield IT — 9 firewalls" is architecturally unique to SAMI's position. Lean into this — competitors cannot replicate it without rearchitecting their tenancy model.

↓

Avoid: "Snooze" on critical behavioral alerts

Remove or demote the Snooze action on behavioral anomaly and credential stuffing cards. CrowdStrike requires human-in-the-loop approval for high-impact actions. A Snooze button at the same prominence as "Force MFA Now" sends the wrong signal operationally — and creates liability for MSP partners.

↓

Avoid: Duplicating CVE alerts across both panels

The current SAMI concept shows CVEs in both "Need Attention" (left) and "SAMI Recommendations" (right). Microsoft Sentinel avoids this by using distinct panels for distinct functions: incidents list vs. entity insights. The two SAMI panels need a clear functional separation — Reactive (Need Attention) vs. Proactive (Recommendations) — with no content duplication.
